# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Active job seekers using Applyline as a daily driver. They open it to log a new application, move a card after a recruiter call, check pipeline health before a follow-up, or scan what's gone stale. The context is focused and purposeful — not casual browsing. They may be at a laptop in a coffee shop, at a desk late at night, or glancing quickly between interviews. The job they're trying to get done: maintain clarity and momentum in a process that's inherently stressful and opaque.

## Product Purpose

A local-first Kanban board for tracking job applications — from wishlist to offer. No account, no server, no cloud sync. Everything lives in the browser's IndexedDB. The product succeeds when a job seeker can open it, understand their pipeline at a glance, and take action (log a contact, move a card, export a backup) without friction or cognitive overhead.

## Brand Personality

Clean, minimal, confident. The tool is not cheerful about job searching — it's steady and reliable. It doesn't encourage or motivate; it organizes and reveals. Tone: direct, precise, unhurried.

## References

Linear — keyboard-first, information-dense, precision as aesthetic. High information-to-chrome ratio. Every element is there for a reason. Dark-capable from the start.

## Anti-references

- **LinkedIn / Indeed** — corporate blue, dense, utilitarian, nothing aspirational. The app should feel better than the platforms it tracks.
- **Trello** — colorful, playful, toy-like. Job searching is not a game.
- **Generic SaaS dashboard** — purple gradients, hero metrics with big numbers, card grids, sparklines everywhere. No self-congratulatory data visualizations.

## Design Principles

1. **Information over ornamentation.** Every visual element carries meaning. Chrome that doesn't serve the data is removed.
2. **Confident restraint.** One accent, tinted neutrals, whitespace that earns its place. The palette is narrow and deliberate.
3. **Density without noise.** The board holds a lot. Visual hierarchy does the work of making it scannable, not whitespace padding.
4. **Dark-capable from the start.** Dark mode is not an afterthought — it's equally considered. Many users will be working late.
5. **Speed as respect.** Interactions are immediate. The tool respects the user's time and does not add ceremony.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA compliance. Support for keyboard navigation throughout (including the Kanban board). Reduced-motion respect via `prefers-reduced-motion`. Sufficient color contrast in both light and dark themes.
